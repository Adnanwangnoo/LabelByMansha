Behind the label

Started at a cutting table, still run from one.

Label by Mansha began as a way to fix a problem: good fabric was easy to find, good fit almost never was.

Our story

Clothing that fits the woman, not the size chart.

Mansha started stitching for family and neighbours before the label had a name — mostly kurtis that needed to survive a real week, not just a photograph. The requests kept the same shape: better fabric than the market stall, but a fit that wasn't a guess.

That's still the whole business. We design a limited set of kurtis, Pakistani suits and seasonal lawn each cycle, then cut every single order to the measurements you send us. Nothing sits on a rack waiting for the "average" body to walk in.

What grew is the range — everyday cottons, occasion-ready embroidery, and now a small bridal edit for the women who've been asking for years.

How a piece gets made

From bolt of fabric to your doorstep

Four stages, in the order they actually happen — nothing here is decorative.

01

Design & sourcing

Each cycle starts with fabric — lawn, chiffon, karandi — sourced directly from mills, then paired with an original print or embroidery pattern.

02

Your measurements

You send your size chart at checkout or over WhatsApp. No standard S/M/L — every panel is cut against your numbers.

03

Cutting & stitching

Our tailors cut and stitch your piece individually. Embroidery and finishing touches are added and checked by hand.

04

Press & dispatch

Every piece is steam-pressed, quality-checked against your order, then packed and dispatched with tracking.

What we won't compromise on

Four things every order is checked against

✦

Fabric you can verify

We name the mill and fabric type on every listing — no "premium fabric" without specifics.

✦

A real size chart

You measure, we cut. If a piece doesn't fit as promised, we alter it free of charge.

✦

Fair-paid tailoring

Our stitching partners are paid per piece at rates we review every year, not the lowest bid we can find.

✦

Honest timelines

Made-to-measure takes 7–10 days. We say so upfront instead of promising next-day and missing it.
